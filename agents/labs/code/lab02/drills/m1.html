A linter is a program that reports likely defects in something without running it, and most weaknesses of a tool definition (a thin description, a vague argument name, two tools that overlap) can be found that way. Definitions are also sent on every request, so their size is a per-call cost you can count.

lint(tools) returns (tool name, code, message) tuples with the five codes in the docstring's order, and the overlap findings come last, after every tool has been reviewed. Take the thresholds exactly: fewer than 15 words is thin, so 14 is and 15 is not, and a similarity of exactly 0.35 counts as overlap. Similarity here is the Jaccard measure: the words two descriptions share, divided by all the different words in both. Compare each pair once and report it against the first tool. words and GENERIC are already written, and definition_tokens adds up count_tokens(json.dumps(tool)).
