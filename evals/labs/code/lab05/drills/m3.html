A statement's citations can fail four ways: a missing document, a quotation that is not there, documents that do not support the statement, or a citation that adds nothing. One "citation accuracy" figure hides all four, so each gets its own rate.

Citation recall is per statement: 1 if it has at least one citation and the cited documents, taken together, entail it (if they are true, it must be true). Citation precision is per citation: it scores 1 only if its statement has recall 1 and the citation is not irrelevant. A citation is irrelevant when its document alone does not entail the statement but the other citations still do. support is 0.0 unless there are citations, every one is ok and the judge says SUPPORTED; then it is the judge's score. An answer's support is the minimum over its statements, since one fabricated citation makes it a liability.

A statement cites [1] and [2]; [1] alone entails it and [2] is about something else: recall 1, precision [1, 0].
