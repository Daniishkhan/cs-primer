When a system may decline, each answer carries a confidence and the system answers only when it reaches a threshold. Coverage is the share of questions answered and risk is the share of those answers that are wrong; the risk-coverage curve plots one against the other as the threshold falls.

risk_coverage makes one point (t, coverage, risk) per distinct confidence, highest first. Equal confidences enter together as one step, because no threshold can answer one and not the other. aurc, the area under that step curve, sums (coverage gained) × (risk at that point); lower is better, and only the ranking of the confidences matters, not their size. max_coverage_at_risk answers the operational question "how much can we answer if we accept at most 5% wrong?", with 0.0 if no point qualifies.

Confidence and outcome 0.9 right, 0.7 right, 0.7 wrong, 0.2 right give points (0.9, 0.25, 0), (0.7, 0.75, 1/3) and (0.2, 1.0, 0.25); AURC = 0.25×0 + 0.5×(1/3) + 0.25×0.25 = 0.229.
