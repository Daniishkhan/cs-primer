A judge's pass rate has its mistakes mixed in: it fails some good answers and passes some bad ones. If you know its true-positive rate (TPR, the share of good answers it passes) and true-negative rate (TNR, the share of bad answers it fails), you can solve backwards for the true pass rate p, because the judge reports p × TPR + (1 − p) × (1 − TNR).

rogan_gladen is that equation solved for p, clipped to 0 to 1. If TPR + TNR − 1 is not positive, the judge cannot tell good from bad and the division is meaningless. The rates, unlike accuracy, do not depend on how many answers are good, so rates measured on a failure-enriched calibration set can be applied to production. correct_pass_rate measures them on the calibration pairs and corrects the production pass rate. corrected_ci redraws both data sets with replacement in every repetition, the calibration pairs (use _tpr_tnr) and the production verdicts, and skips repetitions that cannot be corrected.

TPR 0.9, TNR 0.7, observed 0.6: (0.6 + 0.7 − 1) / (0.9 + 0.7 − 1) = 0.5.
