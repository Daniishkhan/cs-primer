A judge is a classifier made by writing a prompt, so you measure its mistakes against a person's labels on the same answers. Take the person as the truth and PASS as the positive class: the judge can wrongly fail a good answer or wrongly pass a bad one, and a single accuracy figure hides which.

  • confusion counts the four cells and raises ValueError for unequal lengths or a label that is not PASS or FAIL.
  • rates returns accuracy, tpr (the share of human-PASS answers the judge passes) and tnr (the share of human-FAIL answers it fails). A rate over an empty class is None, not zero.
  • cohen_kappa asks what share of the agreement not already guaranteed by chance the judge achieved. If pe is 1 the denominator is zero, so that case needs its own return value.

On a task where 90% of answers pass, a judge that says PASS to everything has accuracy 0.9, tnr 0.0 and kappa 0.0, because chance alone gives 0.9.
