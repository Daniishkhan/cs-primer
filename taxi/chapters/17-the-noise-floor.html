The Noise Floor

Step 14 scored the model on rides it never saw and got one number. Shuffle the rides differently and the number changes, though the model, the code and the data are the same. Before you can say that an idea helped, you need to know how far a score moves on its own.

Where we are

Step 16 ended with two held-out scores that cannot be compared. The straight line trained on 5,093 rides scores 9.32 on the other 1,274 (step 14). The 3-4-1 network took its loss from 284.4 to 1.6 on 200 rides and scored 1.9 on 200 held-out ones, which are not the same rides. A fair comparison needs the same rides and a sense of how far a score moves on its own. Here is the second thing in miniature: twelve real rides, a line fitted on eight and scored on the other four, with only the seed of the shuffle changing. Run it, then change range(5) to range(10) and run it again.

import random

# the first twelve rides of rides.csv: (miles, fare in dollars)
RIDES = [(1.60, 7.0), (0.79, 5.0), (1.37, 7.5), (7.70, 27.0), (2.16, 9.0),
         (0.49, 7.5), (3.65, 13.0), (1.40, 8.5), (3.63, 15.0), (1.52, 8.0),
         (3.90, 17.0), (1.53, 6.5)]
HELD = 4                          # rides kept back to score the line


def fit(train, lr=0.02, steps=500):
    """Gradient descent on the mean squared miss of base + per_mile * d."""
    base = per_mile = 0.0
    for _ in range(steps):
        g_base = g_mile = 0.0
        for d, fare in train:
            miss = base + per_mile * d - fare
            g_base += 2 * miss / len(train)
            g_mile += 2 * miss * d / len(train)
        base -= lr * g_base
        per_mile -= lr * g_mile
    return base, per_mile


for seed in range(5):
    rides = list(RIDES)
    random.Random(seed).shuffle(rides)       # the seed decides who is held out
    held, train = rides[:HELD], rides[HELD:]
    base, per_mile = fit(train)
    loss = sum((base + per_mile * d - f) ** 2 for d, f in held) / HELD
    print(f"seed {seed}: held-out loss {loss:.2f}")
seed 0: held-out loss 2.93
seed 1: held-out loss 1.46
seed 2: held-out loss 1.33
seed 3: held-out loss 1.70
seed 4: held-out loss 1.77

The wall

On the real file the effect is larger. Step 14's held-out loss, 9.32, was the shuffle of seed 0. The same code on the shuffle of seed 3 reports 2.37, and on seed 4 it reports 13.93. Nothing about the model changed, only which rides were held out. An idea that lowered the loss by 1.0 would look like a triumph on one seed and like nothing on another, and a search that keeps whatever scores lower would keep luck as often as ideas. You need a number for how far a score moves when nothing at all has changed.

The idea

Run one recipe under several seeds and look at the scores. Their mean is the number to quote. Their spread is the noise floor: how far a score wanders when nothing has changed. The spread is the standard deviation: the square root of the average squared distance from the mean. It divides by n, as step 9's standardise did.

s̄ = (1 / n) ∑ si     σ = √( (1 / n) ∑ (si − s̄)2 )

Here si is the held-out loss under seed i, and n is the number of seeds. The working rule is one line: a change smaller than σ is noise, not progress. Five seeds give a rough σ, and the courses ask for more seeds and a stricter margin, but this is enough to build the loop on. Step 19 turns the rule into a decision, and step 20 measures the floor inside the search.

Your move

One function. It takes any function run that turns a seed into a score, calls it once for every seed in a list, and returns the mean and the spread.

def noise_floor(run, seeds):
    scores = [run(s) for s in seeds]
    mean = sum(scores) / len(scores)
    sd = math.sqrt(sum((s - mean) ** 2 for s in scores) / len(scores))
    return mean, sd

The library way

The same step in PyTorch run on the lab VM

PyTorch has every piece of this under another name. torch.manual_seed fixes its random generator, random_split is your split, torch.optim.SGD is step 6's gd_step, and std is the spread. The script runs the check's five seeds on the check's 1,500 rides: standardise on the training rides, 100 steps at a learning rate of 0.1, then the loss on the rest.

import torch
from torch.utils.data import random_split
from common import design_matrix, load_rides, targets

rides = load_rides()[:1500]                  # the check's 1,500 rides
X, y = design_matrix(rides), targets(rides)


def run(seed):
    torch.manual_seed(seed)                  # the seed picks the split
    train, held = (s.indices for s in random_split(range(len(y)), [0.8, 0.2]))
    mean = X[train, 1:].mean(dim=0)          # standardise on the training rides
    std = X[train, 1:].std(dim=0, correction=0)
    Z = torch.cat([X[:, :1], (X[:, 1:] - mean) / std], dim=1)
    w = torch.zeros(3, dtype=torch.float64, requires_grad=True)
    opt = torch.optim.SGD([w], lr=0.1)
    for _ in range(100):                     # 100 steps at lr 0.1, as in step 17
        opt.zero_grad()
        ((Z[train] @ w - y[train]) ** 2).mean().backward()
        opt.step()
    return ((Z[held] @ w - y[held]) ** 2).mean().item()


scores = torch.tensor([run(seed) for seed in range(5)])
for seed, score in enumerate(scores):
    print(f"seed {seed}: held-out loss {score:.2f}")
print(f"mean {scores.mean():.2f}, spread {scores.std(correction=0):.2f}; "
      f"torch's default std divides by n - 1: {scores.std():.2f}")

mean 1.08, spread 0.61; torch's default std divides by n - 1: 0.68

The five losses differ from yours because PyTorch's shuffle is not Python's (fed your five shuffles, the same fit gives your five losses), yet the mean and the spread land beside the check's 1.15 and 0.58. The noise floor belongs to the recipe, not to one shuffle. One trap: std divides by n − 1 unless you pass correction=0, so the default gives 0.68 where the population version gives 0.61. Neither is wrong, but say which you used. The library did the seeding, the shuffle and the cut, the gradient (backward), the update and the statistics. You still chose the seeds and the recipe.

What you have now

  • noise_floor(run, seeds): the mean and the spread of a score over seeds. Yours.

The linear model's held-out loss over five splits of 1,500 rides is 1.15, give or take 0.58, so a change smaller than 0.58 cannot be told from a different shuffle.
