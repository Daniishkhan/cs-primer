A softmax policy turns a list of scores, called logits, into probabilities: exponentiate each score and divide by the total. A language model does this at every position over its vocabulary, so the four functions here are the probabilities, their logs, one random draw and the direction that makes the drawn action likelier.

Subtracting one constant from every logit leaves the probabilities unchanged, which is why softmax and log_softmax can subtract the largest logit and never overflow. Taking log of a finished softmax instead breaks when a probability underflows to 0. sample_action must call rng.random() exactly once (the checker counts the calls) and return the first index whose running total of probabilities is greater than the draw. grad_log_prob(logits, a) says how the log-probability of a changes per unit rise in each logit: 1 - probs[a] for the logit of a itself, -probs[b] for every other logit b.

probs [0.2, 0.5, 0.3], u = 0.2: totals 0.2, 0.7, 1.0 -> index 1
with those probs and a = 1: grad_log_prob = [-0.2, 0.5, -0.3]
