A bandit is a machine with several arms, each paying a noisy reward, and the policy is a softmax over one logit per arm. REINFORCE, the basic policy-gradient method, moves each logit towards arms whose reward beat a baseline (the typical reward), because without one every large reward pushes up whichever arm was sampled and buries the signal in noise.

A sample's advantage A is its reward minus its baseline b, and advantages computes it. The leave-one-out baseline (the mean of the other rewards) has no bias because it ignores sample i's own action; the batch mean includes it and shrinks the expected gradient by (B - 1)/B for a batch of B. policy_gradient(logits, actions, advs) averages A * grad_log_prob(logits, a) over the samples. train_bandit repeats: for each sample in turn, draw an action with sample_action and then its reward from bandit.pull(a, rng); compute advantages; add lr times the gradient to the logits.

"loo" gives [0.75, -0.75, 0] (the others' means are 0.25, 0.75, 0.5)
