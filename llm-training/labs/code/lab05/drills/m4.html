A merge's score is a staircase in its coefficients: nudge one and the score either stays put or jumps, so there is no gradient to follow. An evolution strategy climbs it anyway using only calls to the score, here called the fitness: keep a population of candidates, mutate them at random and keep the best.

In a (mu + lam) strategy, mu parents make lam children, and the mu best of all of them become the next parents. evolve starts its pool with x0 itself plus mu - 1 mutants, so the result can never be worse than x0. Children are mutated copies (child j from parent j % mu), and sigma shrinks by decay each generation, so early generations explore and late ones polish. Three rules are checked: sort stably, so on equal fitness the earlier individual wins; call fitness exactly once per individual, on a fresh list; never modify x0.
