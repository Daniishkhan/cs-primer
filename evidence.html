Evidence Log

A private record of what each artifact produced: public code and write-ups, recognition, pull requests and invitations, and coverage. Write it as you go, because at application time you will need dates, links and names, and memory is a poor source of all three.

Why keep a log?

Every artifact should produce evidence of at least two kinds: public work, recognition, critical roles such as merged pull requests or reviewing, and coverage. Hiring panels, and the talent-visa routes some readers will use, weigh exactly these kinds. A log kept as you go becomes a one-page summary in an afternoon.

Where this is stored

Everything you type here is saved in this browser only. Back it up with the rest of your progress from the Review page (Your data, Export).

Visibility actions

Scheduled rather than hoped for. Tick each one when it is done.

  • Month 1: a public GitHub profile with a pinned research-log repository, and one short post per artifact milestone.
  • Month 3 onwards: one issue or pull request a month on a target repository, logged with its date and handle.
  • Month 6: a first write-up submitted to a community venue: a reading group, a target repository's discussion forum, an open-source newsletter.
  • Month 9: specific conversations with maintainers you have shipped pull requests to, asking for feedback on Artifact 3.
  • Month 12: Artifact 4's write-up submitted to a workshop; applications for contract or research-residency roles at small labs.
  • Months 15 to 18: full-time applications, with this log attached as a one-page summary.

Visas: a planning aid, not advice

Talent-visa routes such as the United States O-1, the United Kingdom's Global Talent visa and Japan's Highly Skilled Professional visa each score a mix of public work, recognition and critical roles. Their rules change, and they are not checked here. Ask an immigration lawyer before relying on any route.
