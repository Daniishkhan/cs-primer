If one weight's gradient is typically a hundred times larger than another's, one learning rate is wrong for at least one of them. The fix is to divide each coordinate's gradient by a running estimate of its own typical size. An exponential moving average is a weighted average whose weights fade geometrically into the past.

  • RMSProp divides each gradient by the square root of its running average of squares, so the step length is set by lr and not by the size of the gradient: the first step is about 3.16 lr.
  • Adam keeps two averages per weight: m of the gradient (the direction) and v of its square (the scale). Both start at 0 and read low early on, so divide each by 1 − βt, with t counting steps from 1. With a constant gradient, every step is then about lr in size.
  • Keep ε outside the square root and the state per coordinate, created on the first call.
