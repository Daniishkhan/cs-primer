Weight decay shrinks every weight a little each step, which discourages large weights. For plain SGD that equals adding an L2 penalty (a term that grows with the squared weights) to the loss. For Adam it does not: Adam divides every gradient, the penalty's included, by a running estimate of its size, so the decay each weight feels depends on its gradient scale.

  • AdamL2.step gives the inherited Adam step the gradient g + l2 × w.
  • AdamW.step applies the decay straight to the weights, outside Adam's averages, so every weight shrinks by the same fraction whatever its gradient. The ordinary Adam step sees the unmodified g.
  • Read self.lr at call time, because a schedule may change it. Do not modify the inputs.
