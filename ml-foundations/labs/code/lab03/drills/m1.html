Gradient descent moves every weight against its gradient: w becomes w minus η times g, where the learning rate η sets the step size. The narrow valley is a bowl whose second direction is κ = 100 times steeper than the first, and Rosenbrock's banana is a curved valley with its minimum at (1, 1).

  • bowl and rosenbrock return (f, gradient). For Rosenbrock write r = y − x2 and use the chain rule; the checker compares your gradient with numerical slopes.
  • On the valley each coordinate is multiplied per step by its own fixed factor, 1 − η for x and 1 − ηκ for y, so SGD converges only while η < 2/κ.
  • minimise stops a run whose loss blows up, rather than let it overflow to inf and then nan, so one bad rate cannot crash a race. Otherwise its Trace holds steps + 1 losses.
