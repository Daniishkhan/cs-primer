Multiplying a vector by a matrix again and again pulls it towards the eigenvector with the largest-magnitude eigenvalue (a direction the matrix only stretches). Power iteration does exactly that, renormalising each step so the numbers do not overflow. The Rayleigh quotient v . Av / v . v then estimates the eigenvalue.

  • Stop on the residual |Av − λv| (how far v is from an eigenvector), not when v stops changing: with a negative dominant eigenvalue v flips sign every step.
  • Each step shrinks the error by the ratio of the two largest eigenvalues: 0.5 takes dozens of steps, 0.99 thousands. Equal sizes (+1 and −1) never converge, hence the RuntimeError after max_iter.
  • pagerank models a reader who follows a random link with probability damping, else jumps to any page. The long-run share of visits to each page is that process's dominant eigenvector, scaled to sum to 1.
