Multiplying a vector by a matrix again and again pulls it towards the eigenvector with the largest-magnitude eigenvalue (a direction the matrix only stretches). Power iteration does exactly that, renormalising each step so the numbers do not overflow, and the Rayleigh quotient of the result estimates the eigenvalue.

  • Stop on the residual, not when the vector stops changing: with a negative dominant eigenvalue the vector flips sign every step.
  • Equal-size eigenvalues, such as +1 and −1, never converge: after max_iter steps raise RuntimeError. A non-square matrix raises ValueError. If A v is the zero vector, return eigenvalue 0.
  • pagerank models a reader who follows a random link with probability damping and otherwise jumps to any page. The long-run share of visits is the dominant eigenvector of the Google matrix, scaled to sum to 1.
A = diag(2, 1), v = (1, 1): A v = (2, 1), then (4, 1), then (8, 1)
