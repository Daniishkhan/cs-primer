A vector is a list of numbers, pictured as an arrow. Adding, subtracting and scaling work entry by entry; the dot product (the sum of products of matching entries) then gives length, angle and shadow. The cosine of the angle between two arrows keeps direction and ignores length. The projection of x onto w is the shadow of x on the line through w.

  • Shapes are contracts. Different lengths must raise ValueError (use the given _same_length), where zip would silently drop the extra entries.
  • A zero vector has no direction, so normalise, cosine and project onto it must refuse.
  • Rounding can push a cosine to 1.0000000000000002, so clamp it into [−1, 1].
  • A projection depends only on the direction of w: doubling w changes nothing.
x = (3, 4), w = (2, 0): x . w = 6, w . w = 4
projection (6/4) w = (3, 0); leftover (0, 4) is perpendicular to w
