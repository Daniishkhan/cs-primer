Squared error is what maximum likelihood gives when the noise is Gaussian (bell-shaped). So minimising the negative log-likelihood (NLL) of a line, a loss that also learns the noise level, must reach the same line as least squares.

  • The model is y = w x + b plus noise of standard deviation σ. The parameters are (w, b, s) with s = ln σ, so σ stays positive without constraints and σ2 = e2s.
  • line_nll returns the NLL and its three partial derivatives, as sums over the data. Use the chain rule: the residual r = y − (w x + b) falls by x per unit of w and by 1 per unit of b, and σ depends on s.
  • fit_line descends the mean NLL. Divide the summed gradient by n, or the step grows with the data and the run blows up.
xs = [0, 1], ys = [1, 3], params (2, 1, 0): a perfect line
nll = ln(2 pi) = 1.8379, gradient (0, 0, 2): sigma should shrink
