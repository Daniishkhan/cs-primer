Cross-entropy H(p, q) is the average surprise of a model q on data that really come from p. The KL divergence is the part the model could remove: H(p, q) = H(p) + KL(p||q). It is never negative, zero only when q = p, and infinite if q gives probability 0 to something p allows.

  • A language model's exact KL sums over 100,000 tokens, so training draws a token x from the policy (the model being tuned) and averages a single-sample estimator. With r = ref(x) / π(x): k1 is unbiased but noisy and often negative; k2 is never negative but slightly biased; k3 adds to k1 a term that averages zero, so it is unbiased, never negative and far less noisy.
  • In leash_policy, β sets the leash: a large one keeps the policy at the reference, a small one moves its mass to the best-rewarded reply.
