The 95% in a 95% interval describes the method, not one interval. On many fresh samples from a world where you know the truth, about 95% of the intervals should contain it. That fraction is the coverage, and measuring it audits the interval itself.

coverage(interval_fn, sampler, truth, E, seed) makes one random.Random(seed) and passes it to sampler every time (a fresh generator per experiment would repeat one sample E times), then returns the fraction of intervals with lo ≤ truth ≤ hi.

ab_report assembles the toolkit. The interval for the lift uses the unpooled standard error, because the arms are separate samples; the test statistic comes from two_proportion_z_test, which pools them. Both are right, for different questions. The verdict needs p below alpha and the sign of the lift.

interval (0, 1) always holds 0.5: coverage 1.0
interval (0.6, 0.7) never does: coverage 0.0
100/1000 against 150/1000: lift +5.0 points, p 0.0007, "win"
