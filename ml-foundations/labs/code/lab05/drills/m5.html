A p-value guarantee assumes one analysis of a sample of planned size. Checking daily and stopping when p first dips below 0.05 gives noise many chances to cross the line, because the z statistic of an experiment with no effect wanders like a random walk. To measure the damage, run many A/A tests: both arms identical, so every "win" is false.

  • z_paths computes look i from the cumulative counts of blocks 1 to i, not block i alone. Then each path is a random walk and looks 1 and 2 correlate at about 0.71.
  • end_only_rate checks the last look only, peeking_rate any look; both use a strict >.
  • bonferroni_boundary splits alpha equally over the looks.
  • pocock_boundary is the (1 − alpha) quantile of each path's largest |z|, so at most alpha of the paths ever exceed it.
10 looks, stop at the first |z| > 1.96: about 19% false wins
one look at the planned end: about 5%
Bonferroni boundary for 10 looks: z = 2.807
