A p-value guarantee assumes one analysis of a sample of planned size. Checking daily and stopping when p first dips below 0.05 gives noise many chances to cross the line. To measure the damage, run many A/A tests: both arms identical, so every "win" is false.

  • z_paths analyses each experiment at several looks (points in time). Look i uses the cumulative counts of blocks 1 to i, not block i alone. Then each path is a random walk, and looks 1 and 2 correlate at about 0.71. Draw counts with the given binomial.
  • end_only_rate (one look, at the end) and peeking_rate (stop at the first crossing) both use a strict >.
  • Two cures raise the bar at every look. bonferroni_boundary splits alpha equally over the looks: safe, but wasteful. pocock_boundary is calibrated on the simulated paths, so that exactly alpha of them ever cross.
10 looks, stop at the first |z| > 1.96: about 19% false wins
one look at the planned end: about 5%
Bonferroni boundary for 10 looks: z = 2.807
