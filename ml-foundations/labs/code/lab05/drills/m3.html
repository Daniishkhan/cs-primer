A hypothesis test asks whether a result could be a fluke. Assume the null hypothesis H0 (no real difference) and ask how often chance alone would give a result at least as extreme as the one seen. That probability is the p-value.

  • two_proportion_z_test pools both arms' counts (under H0 they share one rate), divides the observed difference by its standard error under H0, and reads p from the given two_sided_p.
  • permutation_test needs no formula. If the labels do not matter, any split of the pooled values into the original group sizes is as likely as the one seen. Shuffle a copy, split it, and count the shuffles whose difference of means is at least as large in absolute value.
  • sign_flip_test is the paired version: flip the sign of each per-question difference at random.

Both return (count + 1) / (B + 1): the observed result is one possible outcome too, so p is never 0.

B = 99 shuffles, 4 as extreme as the observed split:
p = (4 + 1) / (99 + 1) = 0.05
