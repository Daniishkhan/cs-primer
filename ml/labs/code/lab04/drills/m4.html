Contamination needs only a good memory: the same record, or a close relative, sits on both sides of the split. Scans of one patient look alike and share a label, so a random split puts them in training and validation both; the unit to keep apart is the patient, not the scan.

group_kfold places the largest groups first, which keeps the folds balanced to within one group's size, and never lets a group appear in both training and validation. twin_share measures contamination directly: over all folds, the fraction of validation rows whose group also appears in that fold's training rows. contamination_gap runs the case's own model, case.fit, on ordinary and on grouped folds and returns (random_auc, grouped_auc, share), with the share measured on the ordinary folds. Only a large share with a large gap means the model recalls twins.

a fold validates groups p, p, q; its training rows hold p but not q: share 2/3
ordinary folds: 86% twins, AUC 0.91.   patient folds: no twins, AUC 0.49.
