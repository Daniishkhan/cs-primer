Temporal leakage hides in feature construction: a seven-day mean centred on day t averages days t - 3 to t + 3, so it contains the future. The test is of the code, not the data: change the series from day t onwards and check that the feature for day t does not move.

time_splits makes forward-chaining folds: each trains on a growing block of the past and validates on the block after it, so training always precedes validation. perturbation_test needs no model: for each position t it adds bump to every value of a copy of the series from t onwards, recomputes the feature and compares its value at t with the original. Bumping all later days, not only day t, also catches a feature that reads only tomorrow. temporal_leaks applies it to each of the case's feature_fns.

trailing mean (days t-7 to t-1): bump from day t, its value at t is unchanged
centred mean (days t-3 to t+3): its value at t changes, so it leaks
