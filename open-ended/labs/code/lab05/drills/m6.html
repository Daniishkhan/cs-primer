The best of many noisy measurements is biased upwards: pick the top scorer and you pick the luckiest as well as the best. The experiment measures how much. In each trial, draw fresh questions, draw several designs, score them all, keep the highest scorer, and compare the score it was picked on with its true accuracy (the given true_accuracy). The averaged gap is the inflation. It grows slowly with the number of designs and shrinks only as the question set grows.

inflation(n_designs, n_questions, trials, seed) uses one random.Random(seed) for everything, and the order of draws matters for reproducibility: questions first, with make_questions, then designs, with rng.sample from ALL_DESIGNS, each scored with evaluate. Ties for the top score go to the design drawn first. Return the means over trials: looks, is, inflation (looks minus is) and best_available, the highest true accuracy among the drawn designs, which is the best that selection could have found.

right 60% of the time, scored on 40 questions:
standard deviation = sqrt(0.6 * 0.4 / 40) = 0.077
