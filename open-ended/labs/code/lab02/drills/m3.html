Novelty search drops the objective as the selection rule and rewards behaviour that differs from everything seen so far. A walker's behaviour is its final cell, and its novelty is the mean distance from that cell to its k nearest neighbours: the other walkers of this generation plus an archive of cells that were novel earlier. The archive makes "new" mean new to the whole run.

novelty(cell, others, k) measures the distance to every cell in others, takes the k smallest (all of them if there are fewer, 0.0 if there are none) and averages them. A neighbour on the same cell is at distance 0 and still counts. novelty_scorer(k, threshold) returns a scorer scorer(ends, state). It scores each final cell against the other cells of the generation plus state["archive"], created on first use. Only afterwards does it append every cell whose novelty exceeds threshold. Score first, update after: that order is what makes the archive shape the next generation.
