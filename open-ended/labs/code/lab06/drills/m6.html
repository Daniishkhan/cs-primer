Some results are too good to be true, and a rule can say so without understanding the trick. A good tripwire comes from a bound that no honest candidate can beat. To return the sum of the k largest numbers, a program must read every number at least once, because one it skipped could be the largest, and here reading costs at least one tick per number. Passing in fewer ticks than that means a shortcut.

tripwire(total_ticks, tests) takes the ticks summed over all tests and the list of (xs, k) pairs. The bound is the sum of len(xs) over the tests with k >= 1: with k = 0 the answer is 0 and nothing needs reading. Return True only if total_ticks is below the bound (exactly at it is fine). The harness then scores a flagged candidate zero. A tripwire is a net, not a wall: a cheat that burns a few spare ticks to stay above the bound gets through.

lists of 5 and 8 numbers, k = 3 and 0: bound = 5
4 ticks is flagged; 5 ticks is not
