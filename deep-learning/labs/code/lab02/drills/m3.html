A word pair is scored by the dot product s of two vectors: an input vector for the centre word and an output vector for the context word. The sigmoid, 1 / (1 + e-s), turns s into the probability that the pair is real, and the loss is small when the real pair scores high and every noise pair scores low.

sgns_loss is -ln sigmoid(s) for the real pair plus -ln sigmoid(-s) for every noise pair, since sigmoid(-s) is the probability of "noise". The gradient has the shape of softmax cross-entropy, prediction minus target: the slope on each score is sigmoid(s) - 1 for the real pair and sigmoid(s) for a noise pair; each output vector gets that slope times v, and v collects a term from every pair. Compute every gradient before changing anything, and leave the inputs alone. math.exp overflows near 709, so sigmoid(-800) needs a form that exponentiates only negative numbers.

real pair, s = 2: cost 0.127, slope -0.119 (pull together gently)
noise pair, s = 2: cost 2.127, slope +0.881 (push apart hard)
