Training nudges every weight against its gradient, but gradients differ in size by orders of magnitude from weight to weight. A better rule gives each weight its own step: keep running averages of its gradient, m, and its squared gradient, v, and step by m over the square root of v, so every weight moves at a similar pace.

In Adam.step, per parameter: m = beta1*m + (1 - beta1)*g and v = beta2*v + (1 - beta2)*g*g. Both start at 0 and run low early on, so correct them: m_hat = m / (1 - beta1**t), likewise v_hat (increment self.t first). The weight moves by lr * m_hat / (sqrt(v_hat) + eps); then zero p.grad, because backward adds into gradients. In train, build Adam over flat_params(params) and pass step that step's decayed rate.

first step, g = 0.5, lr = 0.1: m = 0.05, v = 0.0025
corrected: 0.5 and 0.25, so step = 0.1 * 0.5 / 0.5; 1.0 becomes 0.9
