Harness & Evaluator Engineering

An agent that does research is two things: the model, and the harness around it that decides what the model sees, what it may run and how its work is judged. This course treats that harness as the object of study. It covers the ladder from context engineering to harnesses that rewrite themselves, AIDE's tree search over experiments and when it beats a plain hill-climber, the public and private splits and attested results that keep a score honest, kernel engineering for agents, and the benchmarks research agents are judged on. It serves two artifacts: the mini-FML search study (Artifact 2) and the evaluator-hardening kit (Artifact 3).

How to use this course

🌳 A fair fight

Greedy hill-climbing against tree search, paired by seed, with the same budget and an interval around the difference, because that is the comparison Artifact 2 turns on.

🔒 Splits you cannot reach

The score that picks a winner is not the score you report, the evaluator sits outside the agent's write boundary, and the hacking rate goes next to every result.

⚙️ Kernels, read not written

Triton at reading level, and how to tell whether an agent's claimed speed-up survives an end-to-end run.

Chapters are tagged Deep dive, Core or Quick intro so you know where to spend effort. The lab uses Python's standard library and a seeded stand-in for the model, so python3 check.py runs offline, for free, and gives the same answer every time. The workbench runs the real aideml agent on the lab VM and spends a few dollars of model credit. The judges, statistics, benchmark-building and contamination chapters live in A6 and are linked, not repeated.
