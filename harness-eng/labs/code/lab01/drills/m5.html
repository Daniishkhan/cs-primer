Whether a search with several independent starts beats one that climbs a single chain depends on how often an edit helps: when most edits help, one chain is enough, and when most do nothing, extra starts pay off. That frequency is the opportunity density, and it can be measured by proposing many edits to one candidate and counting how many beat it by more than the noise.

realised_density(task, proposer, cfg, n, seed, min_gain) draws n edits of cfg with proposer.improve (the stand-in for the agent) from a single random.Random(seed). It returns the fraction whose public score beats that of cfg by more than min_gain (lower is better). That margin is the noise floor: on 30 public rows a smaller gain is luck, not progress. An edit that crashes still counts among the n, but not as an improvement.

10 edits: 2 crash, 1 gains 0.30, 1 gains 0.04, 6 change nothing
min_gain 0.05: only the 0.30 counts, density 1/10 = 0.1
