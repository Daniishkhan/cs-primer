Whether a tree beats a hill-climber depends on how often an edit helps. When most edits help, climbing one chain is enough; when most do nothing, independent starts pay off. That frequency is the opportunity density, and it can be measured directly: propose many edits to one candidate and count how many beat it by more than the noise.

realised_density(task, proposer, cfg, n, seed, min_gain) scores cfg once on the public split, draws n edits with proposer.improve from a single random.Random(seed), and returns the fraction whose public score is lower (better) by more than min_gain. That margin is the noise floor: on 30 public rows a smaller gain is luck, not progress. An edit that crashes still counts among the n but not as an improvement, so score each new config where a ConfigError can be caught.

10 edits: 2 crash, 1 gains 0.30, 1 gains 0.04, 6 change nothing
min_gain 0.05: only the 0.30 counts, density 1/10 = 0.1
